# Native score-resolution review

2026-10-13. This review uses the registered [nested-shape plan](NATIVE_NESTED_SHAPE_PLAN_V1.md), [anchored-basis review](NATIVE_ANCHORED_BASIS_REVIEW_20261013.md), the [post-study support diagnostic](native_nested_shape_objective_diagnostic_20261013.json), and the completed 90-fit suite (`runs/native_nested_shapes_v1/suite.json`, local artifact).

## Decision

Stop the current shape-family search. The 11-level ordinal PMF is useful only as a TRAIN-support diagnostic; it cannot be the complete author-native probability method. For a complete score protocol, the best next direction is to train the existing bounded CDF family with a proper score on the original scalar labels, without assuming that TRAIN contains the full target support. This is a known objective/model adaptation, not a defensible new mechanism. No novel mechanism or performance claim is supported yet.

The current models were selected on five merged author bins. The degree-6 Bernstein model’s low fit NLL (about 0.945) rises to about 1.389 on shape selection and 1.315 on outer sources; the feature-only `mixture` case’s outer bin NLL is about 1.314. Degree 6 is better than that mixture in two seeds and worse in the third, while hit Brier results are mixed. These 90 fits do not pass the registered gate. The bin score also cannot identify probabilities among labels that were merged into the same bin.

The local TRAIN audit contains 2,722 targets on the 11 float32 values −1.0, −0.8, ..., 1.0. Those are the TRAIN support only. Pre-existing [author-data audit](AUTHOR_DATA_AUDIT.md) and [native evaluation protocol](NATIVE_EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md) metadata record 12 distinct observed CSV scores overall and one held-out TEST annotation of −0.1, which the old five-class adapter could not map. I did not open raw VALID or TEST labels for this review or use that metadata to expand a model support. An 11-value PMF assigns no mass to −0.1, so its NLL there would be undefined (or depend on an arbitrary probability floor); it cannot be promoted as a complete author-native forecast. The five official groups still merge 2/3/1/3/2 of the TRAIN values. The already [registered native-support plan](NATIVE_SUPPORT_PROBABILITY_PLAN_V1.md) should therefore remain scoped to a TRAIN diagnostic.

## Why native resolution matters

The CH-SIMS v2 paper describes a seven-rater process: remove the highest and lowest rating, average the remaining five ratings on the seven-level −3-to-+3 scale, and map that average into the five score groups on [−1,+1]. Its reported evaluation is classification and point regression (including MAE and correlation), rather than probabilistic scoring. The official page confirms the v2 regression range and five grouped labels. Thus the released target is an aggregate annotation score. It does not expose individual ratings, so a learned PMF would forecast the released score across clips; it would not recover rater disagreement or a latent human-state distribution. See the [v2 paper, annotation and evaluation sections](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.02604), the [official dataset page](https://thuiar.github.io/sims.github.io/chsims), and the [official code repository](https://github.com/thuiar/ch-sims-v2).

The present censored CDF forecasts have a continuous interior plus endpoint atoms. Their five-bin likelihood only scores total mass in each author group, leaving within-bin allocation unidentified. A finite-support PMF can diagnose that issue on TRAIN: bin and hit probabilities are sums of point masses. If a continuous model is retained in that diagnostic, turn its CDF into the same 11 TRAIN category probabilities with midpoint cells and include endpoint atoms in the edge cells; fit/select on that discrete likelihood, never on a density value at a label. This is the standard discretized-likelihood control. It remains a TRAIN-only comparison because the existing metadata documents an out-of-TRAIN-support score.

## Minimal fair experiment

Keep the registered 11-level proportional-odds PMF and midpoint-cell likelihood as a source-held TRAIN diagnostic only. It can answer whether five-bin fitting hides score-level structure among those 11 TRAIN values. Use the same frozen fusion context/anchor, nested source folds, fit-only normalization, three seeds and outer-specific point teachers; keep the raw point and clipped MAE unchanged. Do not use this diagnostic, its class prior or its KL projection as the final complete-protocol forecast.

For a complete author-native probability study, keep the bounded censored CDF family and replace the five-bin training/selection objective with CRPS on the original numeric label \(y\in[-1,1]\). CRPS evaluates a forecast CDF at the observed score directly, so it does not require a support list and remains proper for discrete, continuous, or mixed target distributions ([Gneiting & Raftery, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)). Compare only the predeclared degree-6 candidate with the rich same-median `median_mdn` reference, with both trained and selected on that same native-CRPS objective. This changes the supervision target while keeping the registered distribution families, frozen point and source isolation; it is not another formula sweep.

For the TRAIN-only PMF diagnostic, compare the proportional-odds head with the existing `median_mdn` reference integrated over the same 11 midpoint cells and the empirical class-frequency distribution from each fold's shape-fit sources. Score exact 11-score NLL and discrete ranked probability score; derive five-bin NLL/Brier by summing PMF mass. Compute its raw-point hit probability as

\[
P(|Y-m_{raw}|\le 0.1)=\sum_{j:\,|s_j-m_{raw}|\le0.1}p_j,
\]

using the same float32 comparison as the registered event. Report the clipped-point event only as the existing supplemental diagnostic. Show discrete 50/80/90/95% coverage and set width. Exact category NLL and ranked probability scoring are established proper-score tools, not contributions; they apply to this restricted TRAIN diagnostic, not to a claim of full author support.

For that PMF diagnostic only, if preserving the frozen anchor as its median remains mandatory, make a per-row KL I-projection onto the set of PMFs satisfying \(F(m_c^-)\le 0.5\le F(m_c)\), where \(m_c=\mathrm{clip}(m_{raw},[-1,1])\). This is a standard convex information projection onto linear probability constraints, not a new median-preserving mechanism ([Csiszár, 1975](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176996454)). Apply it during diagnostic fitting/selection and retain the unprojected PMF as a projection-cost control. The complete-protocol bounded CDF already preserves its continuous anchor median through its registered construction.

For either study, use the existing TRAIN sources only for source-held selection. Keep the three outer source folds and seeds 13/42/2026; no outer source may enter point fitting/selection, normalization, probability fitting, epoch selection or calibration fitting. For the complete-protocol CRPS comparison, use the same 5,000 paired source/video bootstrap draws and require the degree-6 forecast to improve CRPS over `median_mdn` in all three seeds with the pooled paired 95% interval excluding zero. It must also improve raw-point hit Brier and log score without degrading useful interval coverage/width. If any gate fails, stop this probability-shape line. Do not open VALID/TEST labels or tune target support; after all choices are frozen, any final official evaluation remains a separate one-time gate. Passing supports only an empirical task adaptation, not algorithmic novelty. No downstream action utility was specified, so these results cannot establish a better action policy by themselves.

## Prior-art boundary and transfer

Ordinal cumulative-link regression is classical ([McCullagh, 1980](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/42/2/109/7027621)); categorical/native-support likelihood, discretized likelihood, CRPS/RPS and KL projection are controls. CRPS is the appropriate first full-support score here because it evaluates the bounded CDF on the observed scalar without relying on a support inferred from TRAIN. Post-hoc calibration of frozen regression forecasts also has established precedents ([Kuleshov, Fenner & Ermon, 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/kuleshov18a.html)). A future contribution would need a predeclared mechanism that survives these controls and source-held evaluation; none is identifiable from the current results. Jev can motivate structured decisions and efficient local inference, but no public Jev weights, training recipe, or architecture are claimed or reproduced here.

Do not copy an 11-class head to CMU-MOSEI. Its primary paper describes sentiment ratings on a seven-level −3-to-+3 Likert scale from three crowd judges, and its official SDK documents its own target range and binning. Preserve MOSEI’s native label semantics and use a support-appropriate forecast there; any replication is conditional on CH-SIMS first passing its gate ([MOSEI paper](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1208.pdf), [official SDK description](https://github.com/CMU-MultiComp-Lab/CMU-MultimodalSDK/blob/main/mmsdk/mmdatasdk/dataset/standard_datasets/CMU_MOSEI/README.md)).
