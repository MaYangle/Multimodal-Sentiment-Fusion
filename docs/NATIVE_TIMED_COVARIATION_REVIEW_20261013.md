# Native timed A/V covariation review

2026-10-13. **Recommendation: go for one bounded, pre-registered native point-fusion test only after the standardized-sequence ALMT control is complete and the audio/video time origins are verified.** The hypothesis is that a centered, time-matched audio/vision cross-moment adds information beyond the strong pooled-mean fusion model. This is an empirical test of a known bilinear/second-order operator on this frozen CH-SIMS setup; it is not an originality claim, a causal claim, or a claim that the representation is chronologically directional. Stop the registered CDF-shape line and do not extend it with more CDF families.

## Why this is the only plausible next point-fusion test

The 36-fit [native scalar CRPS audit](native_cdf_crps_audit_20261013.json) completed with 987 checks passed, no audit failures, and 48/96/192-node quadrature agreement. Its pre-registered degree-6 promotion gate failed: the CRPS and native-hit confidence comparisons did not satisfy their required seed/source-bootstrap conditions, and the pooled 90% interval score deteriorated against both mixture and median-MDN. The audit recommends retaining the negative result without promotion. More CDF shape families would continue a line whose registered decision rule already says to stop.

The strongest Chinese native point reference is now the **standardized fusion MLP**, MAE `.411524 ± .003855` over seeds 13/42/2026, versus `.422070 ± .000736` for raw fusion. The raw ALMT adaptation is `.4271 ± .0065`; it is not presently the strongest point comparator. These are official VALID development results, not independent test evidence ([baseline findings](NATIVE_CH_STANDARDIZATION_FINDINGS_V1.md), [native protocol](NATIVE_EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md)). A new sequence mechanism has to clear standardized fusion and a fair standardized-input ALMT sequence baseline before it is worth discussing as a contribution.

The TRAIN-only source-error audit is suggestive but not affirmative evidence for temporal interaction. In the 2,722-row/129-source OOF diagnostic, fusion-256 versus the text-640 control added mean MSE `.011707`; `.003112` (`26.6%`) came from source-mean error and `.008595` (`73.4%`) from within-source error. The decomposition is descriptive, source IDs are videos rather than verified speakers, and within-source error includes all remaining clip-level causes ([source regression diagnostic](native_source_regression_error_diagnostic_20261013.json)). The separate pooled-feature geometry audit reports between-source fractions of total variance of 9.7% for text, 14.6% for audio and 23.4% for vision ([feature geometry](native_source_feature_geometry_20261013.json)). Those statistics describe variation among pooled clip vectors; they do **not** show that within-clip temporal A/V covariance predicts the native scalar target.

Earlier temporal evidence is also a caution, not a positive result for this hypothesis. On historical five-class experiments, the learned window gate had near-zero correlation with its masking loss change, and the small utility-supervision pilot did not improve accuracy (its macro-F1 fell). Those runs use a different target and protocol; they cannot validate a native scalar-regression fusion mechanism ([reassessment](architecture_reassessment.md), [utility pilot](jev_utility_pilot_ablation.json), [source-held gate audit](jev_affect_v2_utility_gate_audit.json)).

## The narrow hypothesis and its limits

For matched A/V rows on a verified common time grid, let `a_t` and `v_t` be projected audio and vision vectors. Test the per-clip feature

`C_AV = mean_t[(a_t - mean_t(a)) (v_t - mean_t(v))^T]`.

The static standardized fusion inputs remain in the point model: text CLS, masked global audio mean and masked global vision mean. The native scalar `M` target, regression head and native scoring stay unchanged. Centering makes this **interaction feature** invariant to adding a constant vector offset to every valid audio row or every valid vision row in a clip. It does not make the entire predictor offset-invariant because the retained global means still change. The paired cross-moment is unchanged by a common permutation of paired A/V rows; it does not encode whether one event came before another or model chronological direction. Independently permuting or shifting one modality changes pairings and is a useful falsification control.

This formula is already in the established bilinear/second-order family. Tensor Fusion Network explicitly uses outer products to represent cross-modal interactions; Low-rank Multimodal Fusion factorizes that tensor interaction. Covariance pooling is established second-order pooling. For temporal modeling, Memory Fusion Network models cross-view interactions over time, while MulT addresses unaligned streams with directional cross-modal attention. ALMT already targets irrelevant/conflicting audio/vision representations under language guidance, and KuDA learns dynamic modality contributions. These primary papers make the operator and broad rationale prior art; the exact centered, time-pooled CH-SIMS scalar-regression instantiation may be a distinct experiment, but this review does not establish a novelty gap. [TFN](https://aclanthology.org/D17-1115/), [LMF](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1209/), [second-order covariance pooling](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2017/html/Li_Is_Second-Order_Information_ICCV_2017_paper.html), [MFN](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/12021), [MulT](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1656/), [ALMT](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.49/), [KuDA](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.865/).

## Timestamp and truncation audit

The frozen `simsv2_full_macbert_raw_av` cache already records A/V sequences and their time arrays under `splits/{train,valid}`. Its metadata has only 2,722 TRAIN and 647 VALID records, excludes TEST from the manifest/cache counts, and binds the source and feature hashes. The shapes are audio `[400,768]` and vision `[55,384]`; the cache also contains `audio_lengths`, `vision_lengths`, `audio_times` and `vision_times`. The corresponding contract is enforced by [`native_raw.py`](../jevaffect/native_raw.py) and [`cmc_raw_cache.py`](../jevaffect/cmc_raw_cache.py); the cache metadata is in `simsv2_full_macbert_raw_av/metadata.json` (`data/feature_cache/simsv2_full_macbert_raw_av/metadata.json`, local artifact). These saved arrays permit a timestamp-aware frozen cache to be repackaged **without re-encoding**. This review did not read feature arrays or NPZ payloads.

The recorded time fields are real-valued seconds, not indices stretched to a common length. Video time comes from FFmpeg `showinfo` PTS after a `2 fps, start_time=0` filter; audio time is the center of each WavLM convolutional receptive field on the decoded 16 kHz waveform ([decoder](../jevaffect/media.py), [timestamp/extraction code](../jevaffect/raw_features.py)). The pinned WavLM convolution has 320-sample stride, so its timestamp spacing is 20 ms. However, the stored audio vector is the final WavLM `last_hidden_state`, whose transformer context can span the clip; the timestamp is an anchor, not proof that each vector contains only local audio. Also, the repository checks monotonic times and zero padding but does not independently verify cross-stream start offsets or A/V drift. Therefore physical A/V synchrony is **not established by this audit**. Before calling any pairing synchronous, confirm the two stream origins/offsets from the source metadata and saved decode records. If that cannot be confirmed, describe the feature as a paired-sequence diagnostic and do not claim physical synchronization.

The cache keeps the first target steps without resampling or interpolation. Metadata reports 69 audio records truncated at 400 steps, with 6,723 audio steps dropped in total; audio raw length reaches 2,132 steps. At 20 ms spacing, 400 retained audio anchors end at about 8.0 s. Vision is capped at 55 frames at 2 fps (nominal grid 0–27 s); one record drops 30 later frames. A valid cross-moment must therefore use only verified overlapping timestamps and explicitly report retained overlap/coverage. Do not pair row `t` in audio with row `t` in vision or stretch either sequence. The source cache can support a first falsification test on its retained prefixes; it cannot recover already-truncated tails without other raw artifacts or another media pass.

The pooled standardization findings fit coordinate mean/STD on TRAIN clip-pooled vectors. For a sequence baseline, apply those exact existing coordinate statistics to valid A/V rows, then restore the original masks, lengths, shapes and zero padding; do not refit statistics over frames or standardize the padding. The text condition remains the provided-transcript MacBERT CLS/global input used by the strong point baseline. The already registered sequence baseline must come first: native scalar ALMT with the same standardized sequence inputs and preserved padded shapes, selected by the native author VALID MAE protocol. Record its larger parameter count and compute; the current cached ALMT adapter does not mask padding, so retain and disclose that behavior for its fair reproduction.

## Minimal discriminating experiment and decision rule

After the standardized-input ALMT run, compare the unchanged standardized fusion MLP with a small projected-covariance extension and matched controls. Every point model uses the same cached backbones, exact TRAIN statistics, IDs, scalar `M` labels, optimizer/selection protocol and fixed seeds. Keep parameter count and training budget equal across the covariance model and its controls; add no unimodal labels or teacher loss. The controls must include:

- pooled A-by-V mean product, to test whether ordinary static bilinear fusion explains the gain;
- per-modality temporal variance features without A/V pairing, to test whether within-clip spread alone explains it;
- an independently permuted A or V sequence, preserving each modality's valid feature multiset and global mean while breaking A/V pairing.

Use a shared low-dimensional projection so the feature and all controls have the same size and parameter budget. Evaluate a common permutation of paired A/V rows as a sanity check: it should leave the covariance output unchanged. Then independently permute or lag A and V within each clip while keeping each modality's global mean fixed. A pairing-based gain should selectively fall under this intervention; if it persists, the evidence favors marginal sequence statistics or extra capacity over matched-time cross-covariation.

Keep official author VALID native clipped MAE/Corr as the primary selection/evaluation protocol, with seeds 13/42/2026 and paired source-cluster uncertainty. This split shares source videos across 646 of its 647 VALID clips and TRAIN, so report the source-held TRAIN diagnostic separately, under its own split and uncertainty; the two cohorts are not interchangeable. A result only justifies further original-method work if covariance beats standardized fusion, standardized-sequence ALMT, pooled-product, per-modality-variance and unpaired controls with consistent seed directions and a paired uncertainty interval supporting the primary gain, and its advantage is independently supported on the source-held diagnostic. If the matched-pair ablation does not selectively remove the gain, or the gain disappears against the static-product/variance control, stop the covariance hypothesis. Even a pass supports only a task-specific empirical contribution pending replication; the covariance formula remains known prior art.
