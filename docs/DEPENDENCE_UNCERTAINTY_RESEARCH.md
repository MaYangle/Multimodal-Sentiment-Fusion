# Dependence-aware uncertainty: literature and falsifiable next step

Pre-experiment research note, 2026-10-12. Scope is post-hoc uncertainty for the frozen-mean multimodal regression runs. The later [registered plan](NATIVE_DEPENDENCE_PLAN_V1.md) and [completed negative result](NATIVE_DEPENDENCE_FINDINGS_V1.md) supersede prospective statements below; this note preserves the literature rationale.

## Project evidence and decision boundary

The current audit reports 18 `native_context_nig_formal_v1` runs. Capacity-matched pooled MoNIG with a joint expert reaches MAE 0.4346, still behind simple fusion at 0.4221. The nominal 90% interval from joint dynamic fusion covers 61.67%; the three-expert dynamic version covers 77.18%. A pseudo-expert is computed from the same input as the original experts. Summing their NIG parameters therefore counts dependent outputs as extra evidence: duplicating every expert can leave the mean unchanged while narrowing the interval. These are the audit results supplied for this research task, not independent literature findings.

Any next experiment should freeze the existing point means and weights. Its endpoint is predictive calibration and density quality; it cannot claim a regression-accuracy gain. The useful question is whether residual dependence between the experts explains uncertainty errors beyond a one-number variance correction.

## Closest literature

**NIG regression supplies marginal predictive distributions, not a dependence model between experts.** Deep Evidential Regression (DER) places a Normal–Inverse-Gamma distribution over the Gaussian likelihood parameters and obtains a Student-t predictive marginal. The [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/aab085461de182608ee9f607f3f7d18f-Paper.pdf) and [authors' implementation](https://github.com/aamini/evidential-deep-learning) support the NIG/Student-t parameterization. They do not define a joint posterior over multiple models that saw the same labels or derived their predictions from the same input. Adding separate experts' `alpha`/`gamma` values is therefore not justified by DER alone.

**Multimodal depression work already combines branch NIG predictions and models branch disagreement.** The September 2026 v3 [EviDep preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16579v3) uses shared, audio-private, and visual-private NIG branches for depression severity. Its evidence score is `e_k = gamma_k + 2 alpha_k`, the fused mean is evidence-weighted, and its fused `beta` adds a branch-mean disagreement term. It also sums branch `gamma` and `alpha` parameters. This is close in task and output family, so a claim that “multimodal NIG branches are aggregated with disagreement-aware uncertainty” is already covered. EviDep does not estimate a source-group OOF residual-correlation matrix between branch errors; its disagreement term is the observed per-case spread of branch means, a different quantity. The arXiv record reviewed here did not link an author code repository.

**OOF residual correlation with frozen means and marginals is already an explicit method.** Zhou and Vanschoren's 2026 [Residual-ICM paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30085) estimates correlations of predictive-standardized out-of-fold residuals from independent regressors, then uses `Sigma(x) = D(x) R_res D(x)` while retaining the independent means and marginal variances. It reports joint-uncertainty gains with little point-prediction change, and requires inner cross-validation for quantities used to make a prediction decision. This is the closest precedent for the proposed correction. Its dependence axis is *different targets predicted jointly*, with a global Gaussian correlation matrix; the JevAffect idea would move the axis to *different experts predicting one target* and must use source-group folds. That transfer is an application hypothesis, not a new covariance formula. The paper is accepted at ACML 2026; no author-code link was present in the arXiv record checked.

**Forecast-combination theory already gives the error-covariance identity.** Bates and Granger's [1969 combination paper](https://doi.org/10.1057/jors.1969.103) uses historical forecast errors to form combinations. For a fixed weight vector `w` and expert-error vector `e`, the exact variance identity is `Var(w' e) = w' Sigma_e w`. The identity can propagate estimated error covariance into uncertainty for a frozen weighted mean; it does not itself establish that a correlation estimate will improve held-out calibration. The classical optimal-weight calculation also changes `w`, which is outside this experiment.

**A Student-t linear opinion pool is an important distinct baseline.** Ranjan and Gneiting study linear opinion pools and show that a nontrivial pool of distinct calibrated forecasts need not itself be calibrated; their [2010 paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2009.00726.x) proposes recalibration, and their [predictive-distribution treatment](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.1638) analyzes calibration and dispersion. Schulz, Köhler, and Lerch's 2026 [deep-ensemble aggregation paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-025-06946-3) compares density linear pools with quantile aggregation and provides [author code](https://github.com/benediktschulz/ADDE). It is univariate probabilistic forecasting, not source-correlated NIG branches, but it makes a strong precedent for evaluating the *whole distribution* rather than relying on intervals alone. The exact mixture of Student-t expert densities is not generally another Student-t distribution.

**Global scale correction is a required control.** Laves et al. propose single-parameter standard-deviation scaling for regression uncertainty, with [paper and author code](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v121/laves20a.html). This can repair broad over- or under-dispersion without estimating cross-expert dependence. If it matches the covariance method, the result supports ordinary post-hoc calibration rather than a dependence-specific contribution.

**Covariance intersection is a conservative fallback for unknown correlations, not a direct frozen-mean correction.** Julier and Uhlmann introduced CI for state estimates whose cross-correlations are unknown in [their 1997 paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACC.1997.609105). CI combines information matrices and generally changes both fused covariance and fused mean. It is useful context for why naïvely treating shared-source experts as independent can be overconfident, but directly importing its fusion rule would violate the frozen-mean constraint and its state-estimation assumptions. Do not describe NIG parameter summation as CI.

## A testable dependence correction

For each training source group `g`, obtain predictions from a source-group OOF model that never trained on any row from `g`. Keep the exact current expert means and per-row mixture/fusion weights fixed. Let `mu_gi` and `v_gi` be expert `i`'s mean and finite Student-t predictive variance, and let `w_gi` be its fixed weight (`sum_i w_gi = 1`). Define standardized OOF errors `z_gi = (y_g - mu_gi) / sqrt(v_gi)` and estimate a shrunken correlation matrix `R = (1-lambda) Corr(z) + lambda I` from training OOF rows only. Predeclare `lambda` or choose it in an inner source-group cross-fit. For a new row, set `D_g = diag(sqrt(v_gi))`; the covariance-propagated variance of the already-frozen weighted mean is

```text
v_corr,g = w_g' D_g R D_g w_g
```

This follows from `Var(w'e) = w' Sigma_e w`; `R = I` gives the matched independence control `sum_i w_gi^2 v_gi`. If the current fusion output is represented by one Student-t, retain its location and degrees of freedom and change only its scale to match `v_corr,g`. Require degrees of freedom above 2 when calling this a variance. For an exact Student-t linear pool, score `p_mix(y|g) = sum_i w_gi t_i(y|mu_gi,v_gi)` by the exact mixture density and keep the same weighted mean; its variance is `sum_i w_gi [v_gi + (mu_gi - mu_mix,g)^2]` when all component variances are finite. Do not replace its log score with a moment-matched single-t approximation.

This estimator assumes that one global residual-correlation matrix is informative after each expert's marginal scale is applied. It will miss correlations that vary by input, group, or sign, and noisy OOF estimates can make it worse. It is only worth pursuing if the experts' predicted marginal variances differ enough across cases for a fixed `R` to produce useful case-varying adjustments. When scales and weights are nearly proportional across experts, the correction reduces approximately to a global scale factor and adds little beyond that control.

## Validation and rejection conditions

1. Build one common train-only OOF prediction table for the existing models. Group by the protocol's source identity so every row/window from one source is held out together; do not use validation or test residuals to estimate `R`, shrinkage, degrees of freedom, or scale. Use the same folds and OOF rows for every control. Any tuning of `lambda` must be inside training OOF. Keep the official validation split for the already-defined model-selection role and leave the final test untouched until this uncertainty protocol is frozen.
2. Compare the frozen-mean baseline, a single global variance scale fitted on the same OOF rows, the covariance propagation with `R=I`, the shrunken-`R` correction, and the exact Student-t linear opinion pool with the frozen rowwise weights. To isolate the off-diagonal effect from average scale correction, fit the same one-parameter global scale `q` on the same OOF rows for both the `R=I` and learned-`R` covariance candidates; also report the scale-only baseline. Do not give the correlation method extra OOF rounds or validation searches. Record its extra fitted parameters and use group-level uncertainty intervals for score differences.
3. Report MAE as an invariant check; it must be identical across all uncertainty-only methods. Separately report Student-t/mixture log score (and CRPS if available), 90% and 95% coverage with interval widths, and PIT or a reliability view. Coverage moving toward nominal is a calibration result, not an accuracy gain.
4. **Support the hypothesis only if** source-group OOF residual correlations are stable across folds/seeds and the `R` correction improves held-out distribution score or coverage-width tradeoff over both global-scale and `R=I` controls, with no change in means. It must also be competitive with the exact Student-t pool. A nominal-coverage match alone is insufficient if the global scale does the same.
5. **Reject the dependence-specific hypothesis** if `R` shrinks near identity, correlations change materially across source folds, corrected NLL/CRPS is no better than global scale or the Student-t pool, intervals become too wide, or gains disappear on held-out source groups. In that outcome, report post-hoc calibration at most; do not claim a new dependence-aware fusion method.

The literature already covers NIG predictive marginals, multimodal NIG branch fusion with disagreement, residual-correlation covariance correction with frozen means/marginals, forecast-error covariance algebra, linear pooling, and post-hoc scale calibration. The defensible possible contribution is narrow: testing whether source-group OOF dependence *between existing scalar multimodal NIG experts* improves calibration beyond equally budgeted scale and density-pool baselines, under frozen point means. It is not novel as a statistical identity, and it earns no claim unless the stated controls are beaten on untouched groups.
